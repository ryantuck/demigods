House Chain Insurance

Pricing flexibility and hard deadlines in a chain of dependent sales.  ← back

The problem

In a property chain, every household must complete on the same day. The completion date is therefore the maximum of everyone's readiness dates, and the chain survives only if every link holds. Both risks compound with chain length — yet nobody prices them. The buyer with a hard deadline silently carries huge timing risk; the seller with total flexibility provides real option value to the whole chain and captures none of it.

The instruments below make that risk transfer explicit. A fuller write-up and a Python version of this model live in the repo.

  • A. Chain-break cover — insurer pays the buyer's sunk costs if any link fails.
  • B. Delay option — the seller commits to a deadline and pays per-day damages past it (voids if the chain breaks). A flexible seller hedges by bridging: completing on their own schedule and renting until their onward purchase catches up.
  • C. Completion guarantee — the seller completes by the deadline no matter what happens upstream. Only a flexible party can write this at a sane price; a hard-deadline buyer is the natural payer.
  • D. Milestone bond pool — everyone escrows a bond and forfeits a daily amount to the on-time parties for each day they run late. Slowness becomes a priced choice.

Reading the results

  • Flexibility is the hedge. The identical delay-option contract is profitable for a seller who can cheaply bridge and loss-making for one who can't. Drag the flexibility slider and watch the two profit tiles diverge — that spread is the yield on flexibility.
  • Hard deadlines have a price. The guarantee premium is what day-certain completion actually costs. A buyer whose real deadline cost exceeds it should happily pay; the flexible counterparty happily writes it. Positive-sum.
  • Long chains are where the money is. Push the chain to 8 parties: break risk and delay tails explode, and so do the premiums — exactly where explicit risk transfer matters most.

Caveats: bridging decisions here use the realized delay (an upper bound on hedge value); link failures are independent (a falling market correlates them); all money figures are illustrative.
